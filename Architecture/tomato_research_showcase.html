Research Findings

Key Observations

A — Color only · 89.06%
  • Highest accuracy among A/B/C — color context is the strongest global signal
  • Bacterial spot 99.3% — water-soaked lesion color contrast is primary discriminator
  • Spider mites weakest at 77.3% — tiny damage masked by background noise
  • Color-dominant diseases consistently prefer this strategy
MobileNetV2 pretrained on ImageNet naturally leverages color-contrast — background carries disease information, not just noise
B — Segmented only · 86.13%
  • Spider mites +12% (77.3%→89.3%) — texture patterns clearer without background
  • Septoria +5.4% (85.3%→90.7%) — spot shapes visible on black background
  • Bacterial spot collapsed −20.6% — color-contrast signal destroyed
  • Leaf Mold dropped −13.3% — yellow-green color was the key feature
Background removal is double-edged — helps texture/shape diseases, destroys color-contrast disease signals
C — Mixed 50/50 · 87.48%
  • Late blight 94.7% — benefits from visual variety
  • Early blight collapsed 82%→62% (−20%) — conflicting representations
  • Overall between A and B — random mixing is NOT optimal
  • Early blight collapse is the strongest justification for Strategy E
Random mixing fails for color-dominant classes — motivates per-class selective assignment
D — Fine-Tuned · 88.42%
  • Mosaic virus 96.4% — best fine-tuned result using combined domain exposure
  • Healthy 98.7% — strong green class recognition post fine-tuning
  • Target Spot dropped to 76.7% — fine-tuning hurt this class
  • Transfer learning from A→Seg domain partially successful
Fine-tuning showed targeted improvement but uneven — some classes regressed suggesting domain mismatch
★ E — Selective Mix · 93.88%
  • Loss 0.1880 — lowest across all 5 strategies by large margin
  • F1 macro 0.940 — +4.7% above Strategy A baseline
  • σ = 0 across 3 independent runs — perfectly deterministic
  • 7 out of 10 disease classes won by Strategy E
  • Spider mites +19.4% over baseline A — biggest single class jump
  • Septoria +12% — structural shape discrimination unlocked
  • Late blight: C domain choice underperformed at 88% vs C's 94.7%
  • Class-aware routing eliminates conflicting visual representations
Class-Aware Selective Mixing proves that per-disease visual domain selection outperforms any uniform strategy. The key insight: disease classification is fundamentally multi-modal — color, texture, and shape carry different discriminative power for different diseases.

🔴 High-Severity Diseases

🟢 Mild-Severity Diseases

Bacterial Spot, Septoria, Spider Mites, and Target Spot all show <5% mean severity. Dataset images likely captured early-stage disease — pixel-level analysis reflects contained infections.

10
Takeaways · Impact

Conclusion

🏆

Strategy E is the Clear Winner

Class-Aware Selective Mixing achieved 93.88% accuracy — a +4.82% improvement over the baseline. Zero variance across 3 independent runs proves the result is deterministic and reproducible, not a fluke.

🧠

Visual Domain Matters Per-Disease

Color-dominant diseases (Bacterial Spot, Leaf Mold) need full RGB context. Shape/texture diseases (Spider Mites, Septoria) need background removal. No single preprocessing strategy is universally optimal.

📉

Loss 0.1880 — Lowest Across All

Strategy E's training loss of 0.1880 is significantly lower than all other strategies (next best A: 0.3366). This validates that class-aware routing leads to fundamentally better gradient signal during training.

🔬

Grad-CAM Validates Attention Quality

19/20 Grad-CAM test samples correctly classified. Attention analysis confirms models focus on semantically relevant regions. B's low attention (18.5%) on Bacterial Spot directly explains its 20.6% accuracy drop.

🌿

Severity Adds Agronomic Value

Beyond identifying disease type, severity estimation enables precision agriculture responses. Leaf Mold (68.5% mean) needs immediate fungicide application, while Bacterial Spot (1.0%) may need only monitoring.

🚀

Future Work

Robustness testing under image noise/blur, real field deployment on UAV imagery, expansion to multi-crop classification, and integration with a real-time severity alert system for precision agriculture platforms.
